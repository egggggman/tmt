# World Guide

## Purpose

The world layer governs TMNT Design Studio's in-universe community experience. It gives rigorous
design work a lived setting, creates continuity, and welcomes participation without becoming
software behavior or claiming authority over TMNT or Magic canon.

The world supports the software by making research and design approachable. It may explain or
celebrate a milestone, but it never changes Magic Facts, Capabilities, Deck Metrics, Findings,
Alignment, or Recommendations. Analytical software remains invisible in-universe.

## Underground community philosophy

The community is larger than its heroes. Residents work, trade, learn, disagree, celebrate, repair,
remember, and create traditions together. **Community First** means shared understanding, safety,
credit, welcome, and community benefit take precedence over status, control, audience growth, or ego.

## Living-world principle

This is community history, not static lore. Events have consequences; corrections remain visible;
businesses, residents, relationships, and traditions may evolve. New details should emerge because a
real story needs them, not because an encyclopedia field is empty. Small recurring details and hidden
callbacks may mature across many appearances without requiring a predetermined ending.

## Software and world boundary

| Software | World |
|---|---|
| Imports facts, derives Capabilities, computes Deck Metrics and Findings, and preserves decisions. | Reports, explains, celebrates, and remembers community life. |
| Uses versioned, deterministic, evidence-backed behavior. | May be atmospheric, playful, or editorial when its form is clear. |
| Produces analytical results from defined inputs. | Communicates results but never changes them or becomes hidden analytical Evidence. |

The world never references GitHub, commits, pull requests, databases, software releases, AI, or being
fictional. It reports the in-world effects of project milestones rather than their implementation
mechanics.

## Canon rules

1. Official TMNT and Magic source material remains the property and canon of its rights holders.
2. Project interpretation is never presented as official canon or rights-holder approval.
3. Published world events form lightweight continuity; drafts and brainstorming do not.
4. Facts, software outputs, editorial claims, opinion, humor, and world continuity remain distinct.
5. Corrections preserve context and state what changed; retcons are exceptional and explicit.
6. No story reveals information that could endanger the underground community.
7. Heroes matter, but the community is never scenery for them.
8. Real contributors are not fictionalized without their knowledge and consent.
9. New world material must serve a real story, reward curiosity, and respect the Constitution.

## The Underground Press

### Mission and identity

The Underground Press exists to **inform, connect, celebrate, and preserve community history**.

> **For Mutants. By Mutants.**

April O'Neil is the **Surface Correspondent**. She is neither editor-in-chief nor the sole face of
the paper. The Press belongs to the community.

Its tone is sincere journalism first, community life second, with quiet humor around the edges.
Reporting distinguishes observation, interpretation, rumor, and opinion; verifies before
publishing; protects vulnerable sources and locations; and corrects errors visibly.

### Conditional sections and features

Investigation, Editorial, Hero Spotlight, and Science & Technology appear only when warranted.
Food, recreation, history, and other subjects may receive one-off features. Sports & Recreation,
Dining Guide, and History & Archives are not permanent sections.

### Milestone publication policy

Publish only when the community has something worth celebrating or recording. Valid triggers are:

- a major software milestone, translated into its in-world effects;
- a major design milestone;
- the first complete Character or Sewer Deck milestone;
- a meaningful community achievement;
- an anniversary or special community event; or
- rare exceptional news.

Do not publish an issue for ordinary bug fixes, dependency updates, minor documentation changes,
routine refactoring, small balance tweaks, or individual commits.

Every issue is classified as a **Milestone Edition**, **Community Edition**, **Special Edition**, or
**Extra Edition**. Preserve its issue number, title, publication date, classification, and the event
it records as community history.

## Puzzle Dojo

Puzzle Dojo rewards attention, learning, and curiosity. It may include:

- a TMNT-themed crossword;
- Sudoku;
- a cipher;
- a maze;
- a logic puzzle;
- a word search;
- spot-the-difference;
- a hidden pizza slice; or
- a reader quiz.

Puzzles should teach, deepen community texture, or invite playful participation. They are never
filler.

## Recurring life

Recurring stories may follow a community problem, a design question, a playtest lesson, a difficult
classification, a contributor's craft, or the changing consequences of an earlier event. Familiar
businesses, homes, workshops, food counters, print shops, archives, transit routes, gathering spaces,
and services may return when stories earn them.

Recurring residents need a clear community role, consistent characterization, and room to grow.
Traditions may include shared meals, repair days, memorials, markets, performances, training
exhibitions, seasonal gatherings, mutual aid, letters hours, and evidence-wall reviews.

## Reader participation

Readers may submit letters, corrections, questions, puzzles, classifieds, event notices, artwork,
story leads, and playtest observations. Participation must be safe, welcoming, edited with care, and
credited with consent. A submission does not automatically become project policy, analytical
Evidence, or world canon.

## Future expansion

Possible future work includes named residents and businesses, evolving maps, oral histories,
community archives, recurring investigations, seasonal traditions, illustrated editions, and
interactive Puzzle Dojo events. Add them only when contributors have a story to tell and the capacity
to preserve the resulting history. The world should grow through use, not speculative completeness.

## Rule of Joy

The world should make people curious, connected, and glad they visited. Humor may soften complexity,
traditions may make work memorable, and hidden details may reward attentive readers. Joy never
excuses cruelty, exclusion, misinformation, or disrespect for source material. When continuity
becomes a burden rather than a source of discovery, simplify it and return attention to community
life.
