# World Guide

## Purpose

The World Guide describes the living community that surrounds TMNT Design Studio. It gives
contributors a shared setting for stories, publications, traditions, and participation without
turning presentation into software behavior or claiming authority over TMNT canon.

For project values, see the [Project Constitution](PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md). Canonical project terms
remain in the [Glossary](GLOSSARY.md).

## Mission

The world makes rigorous design work feel inhabited. It gives milestones a memory, contributors a
place in the story, and readers reasons to return between software releases. It should inform,
connect, celebrate, and preserve—not distract from evidence or turn promotion into analysis.

## Community philosophy

Community First means that the world welcomes participation instead of rewarding insider status.
Stories credit contributions, make context available to newcomers, invite good-faith questions, and
leave space for people to recognize themselves without assigning them a role they did not choose.

## The Living World principle

The world changes through accumulated events, publications, and community contributions. New details
should emerge because a real story needs them, not because an empty encyclopedia field must be
filled. Small recurring details are preferable to exhaustive lore.

History persists. A later story may reinterpret or supersede an earlier understanding, but published
events are not silently erased. Corrections are labeled, and retcons are exceptional, explicit, and
explained.

## Software and World

| Software | World |
|---|---|
| Imports facts, derives Capabilities, computes Deck Metrics and Findings, and preserves decisions. | Gives those activities a human setting through reporting, traditions, stories, and community voices. |
| Must be deterministic where specified, versioned, tested, and evidence-backed. | May be playful, atmospheric, fictionalized, or editorial when clearly labeled. |
| Produces analytical results from defined inputs. | Communicates and celebrates results but never changes them. |
| Uses canonical technical terms from the Glossary. | May use accessible metaphors while linking back to canonical terms. |

World material must never alter a Capability, Confidence value, Deck Metric, Finding, Alignment, or
Recommendation. A story can explain why a result matters; it cannot become hidden analytical input.

## The Underground Community

The Underground Community is the project’s welcoming civic frame: readers, players, researchers,
artists, testers, maintainers, correspondents, shopkeepers, neighbors, and curious visitors sharing a
place shaped by making and learning. “Underground” is an invitation into a community, not a boundary
against the surface or a claim that participation requires secret knowledge.

## Community traditions

- **Release night:** a milestone is explained, credited, and entered into community history.
- **Open table:** unfinished ideas may be shared for constructive questions without being treated as
  promises.
- **Evidence wall:** reports link claims to source material, engine evidence, decisions, or playtest
  observations.
- **Repair day:** contributors revisit neglected documentation and repository health together.
- **Letters hour:** reader questions, corrections, and disagreements receive visible consideration.
- **Rule of Joy check:** work may pause or simplify when rigor has stopped serving curiosity and
  connection.

## Recurring stories

Recurring stories may follow milestone releases, the evolution of one Deck Version, a difficult
rules classification, a playtest lesson, a contributor’s craft, a community event, or an investigation
into a surprising decision. Recurrence builds memory; it should not manufacture drama or promise a
fixed publication schedule.

## Recurring businesses

Businesses are civic settings, not product placement. Stable roles include a print counter that helps
circulate the Underground Press, a repair bench for tools and props, a bookstall or archive exchange,
a food counter where people gather, and rotating market tables for community makers. Names and owners
become world canon only when introduced in a published story and recorded with that source.

## Recurring residents

Residents may include the Press crew, a Puzzle Dojo facilitator, a night courier, an archive keeper,
shopkeepers, artists, players, and neighbors. A recurring resident needs a clear community role,
consistent characterization, and room to grow. Real contributors are not fictionalized without their
knowledge and consent.

## Seasonal events

Seasonal events can include a spring cleanup and archive pass, summer open-table sessions, autumn
story and puzzle exchanges, winter year-in-review publication, and events prompted by actual Magic or
TMNT releases. Seasons are creative frames rather than deadlines or obligations.

## Reader participation

Readers may submit letters, corrections, questions, puzzles, classified notices, event listings,
artwork, story leads, and playtest observations. Publication requires appropriate credit, consent,
and editing. Submissions do not automatically become project policy, source evidence, or world canon.

## Canon rules

1. Official TMNT and Magic source material remains the property and canon of its rights holders.
2. Project interpretation is labeled as interpretation and never presented as official canon.
3. Published project-world events form a lightweight continuity; drafts and brainstorming do not.
4. Software outputs, source facts, editorial claims, and fictional framing remain distinguishable.
5. Corrections preserve the original context and explain what changed.
6. New lore should serve a real story, welcome participation, and respect the Constitution.
7. When continuity and community well-being conflict, community well-being wins.

## Rule of Joy

The world should reward curiosity and delight. Humor may soften complexity, traditions may make work
memorable, and small surprises may invite participation. Joy never excuses cruelty, exclusion,
misinformation, or disrespect for source material.

## Natural evolution and future expansion

The world grows from repeated use. Future additions may include named local businesses and residents,
illustrated maps, community archives, oral-history projects, Puzzle Dojo events, Surface Reports, or
seasonal editions of the Underground Press. Add them when contributors have stories to tell and the
capacity to care for the resulting history.

The [Underground Press Style Guide](UNDERGROUND_PRESS_STYLE_GUIDE.md) governs publication. The
[Roadmap](ROADMAP.md) records possible future directions without turning them into schedules.
