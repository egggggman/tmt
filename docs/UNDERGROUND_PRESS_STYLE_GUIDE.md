# Underground Press Style Guide

## Mission

The Underground Press exists to inform, connect, celebrate, and preserve community history. It turns
project work into useful public context without replacing source documentation, analytical evidence,
or durable decisions.

## Editorial philosophy

Community comes before attention. Accuracy comes before speed. Context comes before spectacle.
Stories should help readers understand what happened, why it matters, whose work made it possible,
and where to inspect the evidence. Editorial independence includes the duty to question the project
honestly and correct the record visibly.

## Writing standards

- Lead with the most useful fact or human consequence.
- Distinguish reporting, analysis, opinion, fiction, and humor with clear labels.
- Link technical claims to canonical documentation, evidence, releases, or decisions.
- Define specialized terms or link the [Glossary](GLOSSARY.md).
- Credit contributors and sources in the form they request.
- Use exact dates, versions, Deck Versions, Rule Sets, and Engine Versions when relevant.
- State uncertainty and known limitations directly.
- Correct material errors promptly without silently rewriting history.

## Tone and voice

The Press is neighborly, curious, clear, lightly playful, and never smug. It can sound like a capable
local newsroom that knows the community well: warm enough to invite a first-time reader, rigorous
enough to earn a maintainer’s trust, and lively enough to make careful work worth celebrating.

Use plain language before jargon. Avoid hype, corporate announcement voice, false urgency, cruelty,
and jokes that require someone else to be diminished.

## Reporting standards

Confirm names, quotations, dates, links, version numbers, and claims before publication. Seek primary
sources for technical and canon claims. Give subjects a fair opportunity to clarify material factual
issues. Separate an observed fact from an inference and an inference from an opinion. Conflicts of
interest and substantial use of automated assistance must be disclosed.

Anonymous sourcing is exceptional and requires a clear community-protection reason. Never publish
private information, security-sensitive details, or personal conflict merely because it is available.

## Treatment of heroes

Heroes are people or Characters with choices, limits, contradictions, and relationships—not brands
or flawless mascots. Celebrate courage without erasing mistakes. Do not manufacture cynicism to seem
serious or flatten a Character to force a deck-design thesis. Official source material, project
interpretation, and playful world framing remain distinct.

## April O’Neil as Surface Correspondent

April O’Neil may appear as the Press’s **Surface Correspondent**, connecting Underground Community
readers with events, institutions, and perspectives above ground. Her reporting voice should be
resourceful, humane, observant, and willing to ask direct questions. She is not an omniscient narrator
or automatic endorsement mechanism. Use must respect official source material and the project’s fan
project statement; project-written reports remain clearly unofficial interpretation.

## Humor policy

Humor should reveal affection, release tension, or reward close reading. It must not obscure a fact,
target a vulnerable person, trivialize harm, impersonate a real source, or make corrections harder to
understand. Satire and fictional notices receive labels when a reasonable reader could mistake them
for reporting.

## Publication policy

Every item has an editor or accountable author, a section, a publication date, a source trail where
needed, and a clear status: draft, published, corrected, or archived. Drafts are not world canon.
Published pieces enter the project-world history described in the [World Guide](WORLD_GUIDE.md).
Corrections identify what changed and when; archival status does not erase the original record.

Rights, consent, attribution, accessibility, and privacy must be reviewed before publication. The
Press does not publish material simply because a milestone or calendar slot exists.

## Permanent newspaper sections

### Front Page

The most consequential current story, presented with verified context and paths to deeper material.

### From the News Desk

Short newsroom notes, corrections, publication context, and transparent editorial updates.

### City Desk

Local Underground Community reporting: institutions, infrastructure, gatherings, and civic change.

### Surface Report

A bounded explanation of surface-world events or project work for a general audience, with links back
to evidence and canonical documentation. It is presentation, not canonical analysis.

### Public Safety

Practical notices about events, privacy, security, moderation, accessibility, or community care.
Urgency must be proportionate and verified.

### Community Board

Contributor news, requests for help, acknowledgments, opportunities, and community resources.

### Arts & Culture

Art, music, storytelling, design craft, source-material appreciation, and community creative work.

### Letters to the Editor

Reader questions, corrections, disagreements, and responses. Edit for length only with permission or
clear disclosure; never alter the writer’s position.

### Classifieds

Brief community notices and playful world texture. Commercial, fictional, and real notices must be
distinguishable, safe, and appropriate.

### Weather

Atmospheric community flavor or genuinely relevant event conditions. Fictional weather never
masquerades as a real safety forecast.

### Community Calendar

Verified dates for releases, review days, events, open tables, and submission windows. A listing is
not an endorsement.

### Investigation

Deep, evidence-heavy reporting on a consequential question. Publish methodology, limitations, and
responses from affected subjects.

### Editorial

Clearly labeled institutional or guest opinion. Editorials use evidence but do not present judgment
as objective fact.

### Puzzle Dojo

Guided puzzles, rules questions, deck-design exercises, and explainable problem solving. Solutions
show reasoning and reward learning rather than insider knowledge.

### Back Page

A memorable closing item: a visual, community celebration, short feature, puzzle answer, or invitation
to participate.

## Milestone publication policy

Meaningful milestones should receive one accurate publication after acceptance, not before. The piece
states the released scope, exclusions, validation, contributors, known limitations, and next learning
question; it links the changelog, relevant guides, and merged work. A release is not described as
complete while its prerequisite PR remains open. Small maintenance releases may use a News Desk brief
rather than a Front Page story.

## Community First and canon

Participation, consent, credit, accessibility, and correction take priority over exclusivity or
engagement. Official canon, objective project facts, interpretation, editorial opinion, humor, and
project-world continuity are always distinguishable. The Constitution’s [Community First and source
material values](PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md#core-values) govern every section.
