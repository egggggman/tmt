## Evidence capture per game

Record:

- players;
- decks;
- starting player;
- winner;
- approximate ending turn;
- whether the game felt close;
- whether either player felt unable to participate;
- one sentence on what was fun;
- one sentence on what was frustrating or confusing;
- whether each player would willingly replay the same matchup.

Also flag:

- obvious rules confusion;
- repeated dead cards;
- repeated non-games;
- cards that strongly defined the deck's identity;
- moments where simulated expectations clearly did or did not match human experience.

## Interpretation guardrails

Human Game Day is not a tournament and is not intended to produce statistically conclusive win rates.

The first session is primarily evidence about:

- fun;
- clarity;
- interaction;
- deck identity;
- perceived fairness;
- replay desire;
- whether the largest Cardcade matchup gaps are visible to humans.

A single loss does not justify a card change.

A repeated human problem should be returned to 🧪 Design Studio as an observation and hypothesis. Design Studio owns any revision. Cardcade may then test the smallest authorized change.
