## Game-day rule

**Do not redesign these four decks before human play.**

Tomorrow's objective is to learn whether the simulated differences are actually felt by players and whether each deck is fun, clear, thematic, and replayable.

Record at minimum for every game:

- players / decks
- starting player
- winner
- approximate ending turn
- whether the game felt close
- whether either player felt unable to participate
- one sentence on what was fun
- one sentence on what was frustrating or confusing
- whether the player would willingly replay the same matchup

## Human-playtest questions

Primary:

1. Does Raphael actually feel oppressive against Michelangelo, or is the simulated 67/33 split less severe in human play?
2. Does Casey vs Raphael feel competitive despite the simulated 37/63 result?
3. Do the four decks feel meaningfully different?
4. Are losses still interactive and understandable?
5. Do players want immediate rematches or to switch decks?

Secondary:

- Are turns moving at a comfortable pace?
- Are rules interactions clear enough without constant reference lookup?
- Are any deck identities missing in actual play despite appearing in simulation?
- Are there cards that repeatedly create confusion, dead time, or non-games?
