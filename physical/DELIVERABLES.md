# Physical Deliverables Register

Mr. Paperback owns physical-product specifications and validation. A deliverable is not complete
because a digital file exists; it must pass the relevant print, cut, fold, fit, durability, and play
tests.

## Sewer Stamps prototype checkpoint

The first Sewer Stamps set is accepted into the Mr. Paperback physical-product suite as a **print prototype**, not a production-final design. It establishes the direction for future sticker work: late-1980s/early-1990s cartoon styling, vibrant colors, deck-specific character stamps, mana-identity cues, and additional turtle/sewer-themed promotional designs.

The supplied Premium Label Supply PLS619 sheet is the mechanical truth for this product. Artwork must be designed for and placed into its physical 2 in × 1.5 in label media; the template geometry must not be changed to accommodate artwork.

The current prototype artifact is `PLS619_90s_sewer_stamps_placed_v0_8.pdf`. Preserve it as historical prototype evidence. The next gate is a physical print/alignment test before any claim of production readiness.

## Field Manual Prototype 0.2 — Skeleton

The Field Manual is now in **Prototype 0.2 — Skeleton**. This stage establishes the physical and editorial chassis before unstable deck and Cardcade findings are frozen into print.

### Design principles

- **Simple first, detail on discovery.**
- The manual must be useful at the table before it becomes a lore/art object.
- Use the established Mr. Paperback visual language: simplified late-1980s/1990s cel-cartoon illustration, thick finished outlines, flat color, minimal one-step shading, readable silhouettes, restrained sewer scenery, and subtle environmental easter eggs.
- Artwork, typography, and symbols should have different jobs rather than repeating the same information.
- Design Studio owns deck identity, decklists, mana identity, and strategic interpretation. Cardcade owns simulation evidence. Mr. Paperback must mark unstable information as provisional rather than invent or silently freeze it.
- Leonardo is the representative interior prototype. Solve one excellent deck spread before replicating the system across the other nine decks.

### Editorial architecture

The manual should guide a player through this sequence:

**Open the set → understand what it is → get ready to play → understand the battlefield/turn → meet the ten decks → learn a chosen deck → play and record matches → discover deeper project details.**

Planned content modules:

1. Front cover / set identity
2. Welcome to Mutants the Gathering
3. What's in the starter/battle set
4. Getting started
5. Battlefield and turn quick-start guidance
6. Ten-deck roster overview
7. Ten individual deck field-guide sections
8. Matchup / league tracking
9. Cardcade / testing background
10. Quick-reference material
11. Credits, legal/fan-project notice, and document version
12. Back cover

### Representative deck spread

The Leonardo prototype spread establishes the repeatable two-page deck chapter.

**Character / identity page**
- large character illustration and environmental scene;
- character/deck name and set number;
- short Design Intent / playstyle statement supplied by Design Studio;
- correct mana identity supplied by the deck source of truth;
- concise thematic identity;
- one subtle scene easter egg.

**How it plays page**
- core game plan;
- key cards / relationships;
- what the player is trying to set up;
- a short pilot sequence or tactical cues;
- strengths / pressures to understand;
- space for later accepted matchup or Cardcade observations;
- quick-reference symbols or reminders only where they reduce reading.

Final decklists, matchup claims, and strategic conclusions remain dynamic until accepted by their owning departments.

### Prototype 0.2 validation gate

Before the remaining deck spreads are produced, create and physically inspect:

1. one front-cover prototype;
2. the Leonardo two-page spread;
3. a complete low-ink pagination dummy showing all 32 page positions.

Evaluate:

- finished booklet size and hand feel;
- text size and reading distance;
- margins, trim safety, and gutter behavior;
- illustration-to-information balance;
- whether the two-page deck spread is understandable without explanation;
- print color and line reproduction;
- binding / folding behavior;
- whether easter eggs remain optional rather than visual clutter.

Only after that physical test should the interior system be propagated to the other nine deck chapters.


## Validation states

Each item progresses independently through **specified**, **print prototype**, **cut/fold
prototype**, **fit verified**, **durability verified**, **play verified**, and **production ready**.
Only applicable states are required, but skipped states must be explained.

Dimensions, quantities, stock, finishes, artwork, and manufacturing methods belong in the owning
item specification. This register records scope, not invented production details.
