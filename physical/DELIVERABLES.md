# Physical Deliverables Register

Mr. Paperback owns physical-product specifications and validation. A deliverable is not complete
because a digital file exists; it must pass the relevant print, cut, fold, fit, durability, and play
tests.

## Sewer Stamps prototype checkpoint

The first Sewer Stamps set is accepted into the Mr. Paperback physical-product suite as a **print prototype**, not a production-final design. It establishes the direction for future sticker work: late-1980s/early-1990s cartoon styling, vibrant colors, deck-specific character stamps, mana-identity cues, and additional turtle/sewer-themed promotional designs.

The supplied Premium Label Supply PLS619 sheet is the mechanical truth for this product. Artwork must be designed for and placed into its physical 2 in × 1.5 in label media; the template geometry must not be changed to accommodate artwork.

The current prototype artifact is `PLS619_90s_sewer_stamps_placed_v0_8.pdf`. Preserve it as historical prototype evidence. The next gate is a physical print/alignment test before any claim of production readiness.

## TMNT metal deck-tin prototype

The current individual-deck-container direction is **Deck Container Prototype 0.3 — TMNT Metal Tin**. Earlier cardboard deck-box prototypes, dielines, artwork, print tests, and physical validation remain preserved as development history and must not be overwritten merely because the active design direction changes.

Physical fit has been confirmed by the project owner: the selected TMNT tins hold a 60-card deck in card protectors. The textured character face remains uncovered. The flat bottom is the deck-identification surface.

Frozen bottom-label geometry:

- measured flat usable tin area: **75 mm × 108 mm**;
- label size: **63.5 mm × 101.6 mm (2.5 in × 4 in)**;
- portrait orientation;
- nominal clearance when centered: **5.75 mm per side horizontally** and **3.2 mm top/bottom**.

The geometry is frozen as the working standard unless a physical print/adhesion test produces contrary evidence.

### Tin-label visual standard

The accepted visual direction is **simple, functional, collectible**. Labels should use a hand-drawn, two-dimensional late-1980s/1990s Saturday-morning-cartoon/comic treatment: flat color fills, visible hand-inked line character, slightly imperfect shapes and lettering, restrained texture, and recognizable sewer/environmental scenery. Avoid glossy 3D rendering, photorealism, excessive gradients, or over-rendered modern packaging effects.

Each label should prioritize a clear hierarchy:

1. Mutants the Gathering identity and set number (for example, 01/10);
2. one large character/action illustration;
3. deck/character name;
4. a short functional playstyle descriptor;
5. the deck's actual mana identity;
6. TMNT recognition at the lower left and Magic recognition at the lower right.

Keep secondary packaging information minimal. Do not add information merely to fill space. The label should be understandable at a glance and should reduce, rather than increase, the cognitive load of a ten-deck product.

### Scene easter eggs

**Every tin-label scene should contain one deliberate easter egg.** The easter egg is part of the environmental illustration rather than a competing information layer. It should reward a closer look without interfering with the character, deck name, mana identity, or other functional information.

Easter eggs may reference TMNT/source-material details, the deck's character or relationships, Magic/deck mechanics, Mutants the Gathering project history, or another established element of the physical set. They should remain subtle enough that discovering them is optional. Record the intended easter egg with the artwork/prototype so it remains reproducible and does not become accidental or undocumented canon.

### Easter-egg creative policy

The easter-egg layer is intentionally **surprise-first** for the project owner. During normal design review, present the finished scene without explaining the hidden reference unless explanation is requested. The design record should still preserve the intended reference so it is not lost.

Across the ten labels, vary the depth and subject of the hidden material rather than repeating one kind of joke. Suitable sources include TMNT/source-material knowledge, character relationships, deck mechanics, Magic visual language, and Mutants the Gathering's own development history (including earlier physical prototypes and project departments). The complete ten-label set may therefore function as a quiet visual history of the project for players who look closely.

The easter egg remains subordinate to usability: one primary hidden reference per scene, naturally embedded in the environment, never another required badge or information block, and never allowed to compromise character readability, deck identification, mana identity, or the simple/functional/collectible principle.

The current Leonardo hand-drawn label iteration is the visual-reference checkpoint for this direction. It is a visual standard, not yet a production-final print artifact. The next gate is to turn the approved direction into a correctly sized 63.5 mm × 101.6 mm print proof and physically test placement, adhesion, readability, and finish on an actual tin.

## Validation states

Each item progresses independently through **specified**, **print prototype**, **cut/fold
prototype**, **fit verified**, **durability verified**, **play verified**, and **production ready**.
Only applicable states are required, but skipped states must be explained.

Dimensions, quantities, stock, finishes, artwork, and manufacturing methods belong in the owning
item specification. This register records scope, not invented production details.
