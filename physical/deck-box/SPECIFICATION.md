# Deck Box Specification

## Accepted starting point

Begin with a simple, durable box that can be printed, cut, folded, and assembled at home. The supplied
physical box image is the structural reference for the first prototype. It does not by itself decide
dimensions, panel orientation, artwork placement, tolerances, stock, closure, or final dieline.

## Undecided fields

- internal and external dimensions;
- card orientation and face orientation;
- panel layout and artwork placement;
- bleed, safe area, cut, score, tab, and glue geometry;
- paper or board stock and finish;
- closure and opening behavior;
- sleeved-card capacity and accessory allowance;
- final dieline and printer settings.

These fields must be measured or tested. They must not be reconstructed from memory or inferred from
the reference image alone.

## Prototype gates

1. **Print:** scale and marks reproduce correctly on the chosen printer and stock.
2. **Cut and fold:** cuts, scores, tabs, and assembly are understandable and repeatable.
3. **Fit:** the intended sleeved or unsleeved deck and any approved inserts fit without damage.
4. **Durability:** seams, edges, closure, and surfaces survive representative handling.
5. **Play use:** setup, deck access, storage, and table use are practical.

Artwork and final face design begin only after the structural prototype passes its relevant gates.
Record each prototype version, measurements, materials, observed failures, and next change.
